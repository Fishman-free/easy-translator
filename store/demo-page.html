The Quiet Craft of Reading in a Second Language

A short essay on why dictionaries should disappear into the background

Reading in a second language is a peculiar kind of patience. Your eyes move faster than your understanding, and every unfamiliar word asks you to stop, look it up, and lose the thread of the sentence you were holding.

The serendipity of a good dictionary is that it rewards curiosity without interrupting it. Look up a word and you should come back to the page a few seconds later, still carrying the sentence in your head.

That is why a dictionary belongs at the edge of your attention: available the moment you hesitate, invisible when you don't. A tool that demands a click, a tab, or a selection is a tool that keeps reminding you that you are reading a foreign language.

Slower readers are not worse readers. They are simply building a second vocabulary, one word at a time — and every tool that removes friction makes that work a little more like reading, and a little less like studying.
